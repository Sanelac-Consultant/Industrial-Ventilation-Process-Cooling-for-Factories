### Nameplate Power Is Not the Heat Load

A 90 kW motor does not dump 90 kW into the room. Part leaves as shaft work, part returns with cooling water, and part radiates from casings. Designers walk the floor with an infrared thermometer and a clamp meter, noting duty cycles and hot surfaces such as furnace doors and uninsulated steam lines. The diversity-adjusted figure is frequently a third lower than the sum of nameplates, so fans and chillers shrink and run closer to their efficient range.

Radiant heat needs separate treatment. Operators beside a heat-treatment line feel radiant temperature, not air temperature, so air showers at workstations, reflective shields, and insulated roof sheets with ridge ventilators work better than cooling the whole hall. A bare metal roof can touch 60°C before the monsoon, so roof treatment alone can take several degrees off the floor.

### Zoning Air Into General, Local and Make-Up Streams

Industrial air handling works in three layers, and mixing them up is the most common design error.

* General dilution ventilation removes diffuse heat, using high-level exhaust and low-level louvers so the stack effect does most of the work.
* Local exhaust captures welding fume, grinding dust, or oil mist at the source, with capture velocities of roughly 0.5 to 2.5 m/s depending on how actively the contaminant is generated.
* Make-up air replaces whatever the exhaust removes, filtered and, where the process demands it, tempered.

Make-up air is the layer people skip. Exhaust 20,000 m³/h without replacing it and the building goes negative: doors slam, gas burners backdraft, and dust is dragged through every gap in the cladding. Fans are then selected on the real system curve, with a VFD, because filters load up and the operating point drifts within weeks.

### Process Cooling Loops Built Around Temperature, Not Habit

Hydraulic oil coolers, compressor intercoolers and furnace jackets can usually run on cooling tower water at 30 to 35°C. Injection moulding, laser cutting and precision machining typically need chilled water between 7 and 12°C. Putting both on one chiller is a habit, and an expensive one. A capable [**HVAC Consultant Faridabad**](https://sanelac.com/service/hvac-consultant/) engineers trust will split the loops, so the chiller serves only the loads that truly need it while the tower carries the rest.

Air-cooled chillers should be selected for 46 to 47°C ambient, not the 35°C standard rating point, because capacity falls steeply in the hottest week, exactly when production peaks. Where lines run around the clock, N+1 redundancy and a plate heat exchanger for free cooling from December to February let the plant keep chillers off for long stretches.

### Local Conditions That Quietly Change the Design

Water in this region is hard, and hard water scales cooling towers and plate exchangers faster than most budgets anticipate. Softened make-up, conductivity-based blowdown and side-stream filtration keep approach temperatures honest. Dust is the second factor: G4 pre-filters ahead of finer F7 stages on make-up units protect coils and spare the maintenance crew from weekly cleaning.

Weather finishes the picture. Direct evaporative cooling works well in the dry heat of April to June, then fades once July humidity pushes the design wet-bulb near 28°C. Plants relying on it alone are comfortable in May and miserable in August, so a hybrid with supplemental fans or a small chilled-water coil is wiser.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. Does a factory need separate ventilation and process cooling designs?**
Yes. Ventilation controls air quality and worker heat exposure, while process cooling protects machines and product quality. They share heat rejection and power infrastructure, so they should be coordinated, but sizing, controls and redundancy follow different logic and should never be copied from one to the other.

**Q2. Which cooling method suits a Faridabad factory in peak summer?**
Water-cooled systems with a cooling tower handle peak summer best. Air-cooled chillers lose capacity above 45°C, and direct evaporative cooling struggles once monsoon humidity arrives. Most plants end up with a hybrid: tower water for hot loads, chilled water for precision loads.

**Q3. Can waste heat from process cooling be reused?**
Yes. Condenser and compressor heat can preheat boiler feed water, wash tanks, or canteen water, cutting fuel use. The economics work best where hot-water demand runs continuously, so the design should match recovery capacity to the plant’s actual hot-water profile.
